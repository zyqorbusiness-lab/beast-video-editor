# Study: editingworld8755 - Raw vs Edit showcase

- Reel: https://www.instagram.com/editingworld8755/reel/DZgzUHxSLBG/
- 1080x1920 @ 60fps (native), 23.00s, 1380 frames extracted at native
  cadence (23 contact sheets). Originally studied from a 690-frame 30fps
  sample; re-extracted at 60fps on 2026-11-19. Sheets 1, 17 and 23
  re-viewed at native cadence; the scene inventory below comes from the
  earlier full viewing of the 30fps sample.
- Audio: 63 onsets, ~83.7 BPM estimate (rough onset-density estimate;
  the earlier ~200 BPM figure came from the 30fps-sampled run and was
  not reliable)

## What it is

A before/after editing showcase: raw clip vs the same clip fully treated,
used as proof of skill. Orange is the brand accent.

## Techniques observed

- **Orange brand world.** Flat orange backgrounds with prop photography
  (dice) establish the brand before any footage plays.
- **Green-highlight caption words.** Captions highlight the active word in
  green - a second accent reserved exclusively for the caption system, so it
  never fights the orange brand.
- **Masked subject pop-out over graphics.** The subject is cut out and
  popped over a graphic arrow - the same mask-layer family as metros'
  text-behind-subject, used for emphasis instead of integration.
- **Flashlight / spotlight mask reveal.** A circular spotlight mask sweeps
  to reveal the "after" state - a reveal transition that is diegetic
  (motivated by the content) rather than a generic wipe.
- **Colour-flash transitions.** Full-frame colour flashes mark the raw/edit
  switches, so the comparison reads instantly.
- **Handwritten script on beige card.** A texture break: casual script font
  on a warm card, used for commentary beats.

## Numbers worth stealing

- Showcase structure: hook (brand world) -> raw -> edit -> breakdown -> CTA
- Two-accent discipline: brand accent (orange) + system accent (green),
  never mixed in the same element
- Full-frame flash transition measured at native cadence: 4 frames
  (frames 966-969 at 60fps, detected as a bright full-frame run)
